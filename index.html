Airin Akter

PhD Candidate in Geography, University of Florida

I study how rain is spread around tropical cyclones that make landfall in the North Indian Ocean. I use satellite rainfall data, weather reanalysis, and machine learning to find what shapes these rainfall patterns. My goal is to improve how we understand and predict cyclone rainfall and its flood risk.

Before my PhD, I worked in GIS and humanitarian mapping, including community-based mapping for cyclone evacuation planning in coastal Bangladesh.

Research

My dissertation looks at the structure of tropical cyclone rainfall in the North Indian Ocean. It is advised by Dr. Corene Matyas in the Department of Geography at the University of Florida. The work has three parts.

1. Comparing satellite rainfall datasets

Which rainfall datasets can we trust for cyclones that make landfall in this region? I compare two widely used products, IMERG and MSWEP, to see how well each one captures cyclone rainfall.

2. Environmental controls across landfall stages

How does rainfall structure change as a cyclone moves toward land and crosses the coast? I study how the surrounding environment, such as moisture and wind shear, shapes the size, shape, and spread of rainfall at each stage.

3. Predicting rainfall structure with machine learning

Can we predict rainfall structure from the environmental conditions around a storm? I use explainable machine learning to predict rainfall patterns and to show which environmental factors matter most.

Methods and tools

  • Satellite precipitation data (IMERG, MSWEP) and ERA5 reanalysis
  • Spatial metrics of tropical cyclone rainfall
  • Machine learning with explainability methods
  • Python, GIS, and high-performance computing
